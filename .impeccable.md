## Design Context

### Users

TradeFlow serves wholesale and distribution teams working across office,
warehouse, and delivery environments. Primary users include sales
representatives and managers, warehouse clerks, delivery staff, purchasing
officers, finance staff, and operations administrators. They use the web
console for dense review, entry, approvals, configuration, and investigation,
and the mobile app for focused customer, stock, pick, delivery, evidence, and
offline-draft tasks. The interface should help experienced operators complete
high-frequency work quickly while making authorization, custody, payment, and
posting state unmistakable.

### Brand Personality

Precise, dependable, and composed. TradeFlow should create confidence that
operational details are controlled and auditable without feeling bureaucratic
or cold. Its voice is direct, calm, specific, and respectful—especially when a
user is blocked, offline, or correcting an exception.

### Aesthetic Direction

A light-first industrial/editorial operations console: structured like a
well-made dispatch document, with disciplined rules, strong information
hierarchy, compact data where density helps, and generous separation where a
decision needs focus. Use warm paper-tinted neutrals, deep ink/navy, a
restrained safety-orange accent, and distinct semantic status colors. Prefer
purposeful typography, tabular numerals, crisp dividers, and flattened
information architecture over generic card grids.

Web should feel like a precise control desk; mobile should feel like a durable
field instrument with large targets, clear next actions, safe-area handling,
and resilient offline/sync states. Avoid neon cyan or purple, gradients,
glassmorphism, excessive rounded cards and shadows, consumer-playful styling,
decorative charts, and color-only status communication.

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard operation, visible focus, 200% browser zoom,
large text, screen readers, reduced motion, color-vision differences, and
44-point minimum touch targets. Motion should be rare, brief, and explain a
state change.

### Design Principles

1. **Operational state before decoration.** Make the current status, custody,
   authority, payment condition, sync state, and next valid action immediately
   legible.
2. **Dense, never cramped.** Use tables and compact controls for expert review,
   but preserve strong grouping, readable measures, and touch-ready targets.
3. **One concept, adapted surfaces.** Share terminology, tokens, and outcomes
   across web and mobile while adapting layout to office and field tasks.
4. **Exceptions deserve clarity.** Explain what happened, why work cannot
   proceed, and the exact recovery action without blame or vague errors.
5. **Trust is visible.** Preserve correlation details, authoritative server
   acknowledgement, offline/pending distinctions, and color-independent status
   cues wherever they affect a business decision.
